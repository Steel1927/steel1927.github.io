How take-home pay is calculated in Canada (2026)

Take-home pay = salary − federal income tax − provincial or territorial income tax − CPP (QPP in Quebec) − EI (− QPIP in Quebec).

The lowest federal rate is 14% for all of 2026, down from 15%. The federal basic personal amount is $16,452.

CPP and EI 2026

  • CPP: 5.95% on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 (max $4,230.45), plus CPP2 at 4% between $74,600 and $85,000 (max $416).
  • EI: 1.63% up to $68,900 of insurable earnings (max $1,123.07). In Quebec, 1.30% (max $895.70).
  • Quebec: QPP 6.30% (max $4,479.30) and QPIP 0.43% (max $442.90). Federal tax is reduced by the 16.5% Quebec abatement.

Provincial notes

Ontario adds a surtax and the Ontario Health Premium (up to $900). British Columbia raised its lowest rate to 5.60% for 2026. Newfoundland and Labrador raised its basic personal amount to $13,094. PEI added a 20% bracket above $200,000.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $60,000 after tax in Ontario in 2026?

About $47,340 a year ($3,945 a month): $5,338 federal tax, $2,982 Ontario tax including the health premium, $3,362 CPP and $978 EI.

What is the federal basic personal amount for 2026?

$16,452 for net incomes up to $181,440. It gradually drops to $14,829 at $258,482 and above.

Does an RRSP contribution reduce my taxes?

Yes. RRSP contributions are deducted from taxable income, so they lower federal and provincial income tax, but not CPP or EI.
