How your 2026 paycheck is calculated

Take-home pay = gross pay − federal income tax − Social Security − Medicare − state (and local) income tax − state payroll deductions such as SDI or paid family leave − pre-tax deductions such as 401(k) and health premiums.

2026 standard deduction: $16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household. The Child Tax Credit is $2,200 per child under 17.

FICA 2026

Social Security: 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 (max $11,439). Medicare: 1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly). Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce income tax but not FICA.

State taxes

Nine states do not tax wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. Several states cut rates for 2026, including Georgia (4.99%), Utah (4.45%), Arkansas (3.7% top), South Carolina and West Virginia. California, New York, New Jersey, Rhode Island and Hawaii withhold disability insurance. Washington, Massachusetts, Oregon, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and Minnesota withhold paid family and medical leave premiums.

This is an annual estimate using the standard deduction. Local income taxes other than New York City (for example Maryland counties, Pennsylvania and Ohio cities) are not included.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $75,000 after taxes in 2026?

For a single filer in a state with no income tax, such as Texas or Florida, take-home pay is about $61,590 a year: $7,670 federal income tax and $5,738 FICA. State income tax reduces it further in most other states.

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

$16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $24,150 for heads of household.

How much is the Child Tax Credit in 2026?

$2,200 per qualifying child under 17. It phases out above $200,000 of income ($400,000 married filing jointly).

Why is my paycheck different?

Payroll withholding follows your W-4 and pay frequency, so it can differ from your final annual tax. Local taxes, benefits and other deductions also change it.
