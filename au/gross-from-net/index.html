How take-home pay is calculated in Australia (2026–27)

Take-home pay = salary − income tax (after the Low Income Tax Offset) − Medicare levy − HELP repayments. Superannuation (12%) is paid by your employer on top of your salary, unless your pay is quoted as a package including super.

The bottom rate falls from 16% to 15% from 1 July 2026 (and to 14% from 1 July 2027), which saves up to $268 a year.

Low Income Tax Offset

Up to $700 for taxable income up to $37,500. It reduces by 5c per $1 up to $45,000, then by 1.5c per $1, reaching zero at $66,667.

Medicare levy and HELP

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income, with a reduction for low incomes. HELP repayments use the marginal system from 2026–27: nothing up to $69,528, 15c per $1 above that, 17c per $1 above $129,717, and 10% of total income from $186,051.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $80,000 after tax in 2026–27?

About $63,880 a year ($5,323 a month): $14,520 income tax and $1,600 Medicare levy, with no HELP debt. Your employer also pays $9,600 super on top.

What is the tax-free threshold?

$18,200 for Australian residents. Foreign residents have no tax-free threshold.

Is super taken out of my salary?

No. The 12% Superannuation Guarantee is paid by your employer on top of your salary. If your job is advertised as a package "including super", choose that option above.
