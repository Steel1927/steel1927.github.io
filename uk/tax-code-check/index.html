Why 5.6 million people overpay through their tax code

PAYE tax is only as good as the code your employer holds. Emergency codes after a job change, a BR code on an only job, benefits in kind that ended years ago, or two jobs sharing one allowance all lead to the wrong deduction — and HMRC has no duty to tell you. UHY Hacker Young estimated 5.6 million people overpaid around £3.5bn in a single year because of wrong codes. Refunds are yours to claim for up to four tax years.

How the check works

Payroll works period by period. For a code such as 1257L it gives you free pay of (1257 × 10 + 9) ÷ 12 = £1,048.25 a month, rounds the rest down to the pound and applies the 2026/27 bands (20% up to £3,141.67 a month, 40% up to £10,428.33, 45% above; Scotland uses its own six bands). This tool repeats that calculation for the code you enter and compares it with the tax on your payslip. If the numbers disagree, it searches every code from BR to K2500 to find the one payroll actually applied. National Insurance is checked at the category A employee rate: 8% between £1,048 and £4,189 a month, 2% above.

What to do if the tax is wrong

  1. Payroll used a different code from the one printed? Ask your payroll team which code they hold and when it was received. They must use the latest code HMRC sent (a P6 or P9 notice).
  2. Code is applied correctly but looks wrong for you? Sign in to your personal tax account and use "Check your Income Tax for the current year" to see how HMRC built the code and change wrong estimates of benefits or other income. Or call HMRC on 0300 200 3300 with your National Insurance number.
  3. Started a new job? Hand over your P45 or complete the starter checklist. Choose statement A (only job since 6 April) or B (had another job) correctly — statement C puts you on BR.
  4. Overpaid this year? Once HMRC issues a cumulative code, payroll refunds the excess in your next payslip. Otherwise HMRC sends a P800 calculation after 5 April and pays the refund to your bank via the personal tax account.
  5. Overpaid in earlier years? You can claim back to the 2022/23 tax year until 5 April 2027. Do not pay a refund company a percentage to do this — the form is free.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my tax code BR?

BR means all your pay is taxed at 20% with no Personal Allowance. It is correct for a second job or a pension, but on your only job it usually means your employer had no P45 or starter checklist. On £3,000 a month it costs about £210 a month more than 1257L until HMRC corrects it.

What does W1, M1 or X after my code mean?

The code is being used on a non-cumulative "week 1 / month 1" basis: each pay period is taxed on its own, so earlier overpayments are not refunded through payroll. It is normal after a job change and should be replaced by a cumulative code within weeks.

My payslip tax does not match any code — why?

The most common reason is a cumulative catch-up: after a code change, payroll adjusts the whole year to date in one payslip, so one period is unusually high or low. Enter your tax period number and year-to-date figures to check the cumulative position instead.

How do I get a tax refund from HMRC?

If the code is fixed during the year, payroll refunds the excess automatically. After the tax year ends, HMRC sends a P800 letter; you claim the refund through your personal tax account or the HMRC app. You can go back four tax years.

Does this check National Insurance too?

Yes. Enter the NI deducted and the tool compares it with the category A employee rate for 2026/27 (8% between £1,048 and £4,189 a month, 2% above). Directors, people over State Pension age and some under-21 or apprentice categories use different rules.
